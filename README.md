# 3d_terrain — a 3-D terrain viewer for Kartverket's 1 m model

Updated 2026-10-17 (evening).

A web page that draws a 1 m terrain model (DTM) of any place in Norway in 3-D, with the sun and its shadows for any date and time, and two Python scripts that package a place — its terrain, a draped map, cliff areas and your own points — as a folder the page reads or as one self-contained HTML file that opens offline.

**Live viewer:** https://pcmoan70.github.io/3d_terrain/ — it opens on a flat map of Norway: **tap a place, choose the model (1 m, radius ≤ 5 km; 10 m, radius ≤ 15 km; or a 1 m centre of ≤ 5 km inside a 10 m surround of ≤ 15 km) and the radius, Build 3-D view**. A 1 m square dragged past the 1 m limit (5 km radius) flips to the mixed model by itself — the inner frame (1 m, at that limit) appears inside the outer (10 m), each labelled at its corner; the same happens to a `?r=` beyond 5 km at 1 m. The terrain is fetched straight from Kartverket's model (the Geonorge WCS) and shown, every slow step (fetching, reading the tiles, filling missing ground, the map, the sun) with a progress bar; `?lat=&lon=&r=&res=` opens the same view by link, and **Share…** makes that link. The link may carry points to draw: `#pts=<URL-encoded JSON>`, an array of `[lat, lon, name, text, colour]` (text and colour optional, colour any CSS colour) — they become the overlay file *Points from the link* under Map, a pin each, name and text in the popup on a tap; this is how BirdsWhere's **3-D terrain** button sends the dots on its map along. **A map-built view grows from a tap:** the popup on the terrain offers the model (*1 m*, *10 m*, *1 m centre + 10 m around*) and, on one line under it, three buttons — **Fetch+** fetches the tiles round the tapped point (those already in hand are not fetched again) and shows everything fetched so far as one view, the bounding square of the union of all the squares asked for (its centre and radius follow; ground no square covers is the grey "missing DTM" fill; the radius stops at 15 km, or 5 km with 1 m tiles only); **Fetch** moves the view to that square alone, at the chosen model, reusing the tiles in hand. At *1 m centre + 10 m around*, the 1 m centre round the tapped point is the view's own 1 m radius if it was built with one, else the 1 m model's full reach (5 km) — fetched at 1 m even where 10 m tiles already cover it. 1 m and 10 m tiles together draw as one 1 m square about the centre (≤ 5 km, reaching the 1 m squares) in a 10 m surround — 1 m ground farther out shows at the coarse grid. **Save** then writes the whole view (every tile shown). Each 1 m tile is 4 MB in the browser's memory, so a dozen 1 m fetches is a few hundred MB. The demo folder is Preikestolen.

Everything happens in the browser: the page reads GeoTIFF tiles, mosaics and smooths them, builds the mesh, drapes the map, marches the sun. There is no server and no build step.

## The viewer (`viewer/viewer3d_template.html` → `site/index.html`)

Draws a terrain model from a **folder** (radius up to 5 km: beyond 1.5 km the grid coarsens to ⌈2R / 3000⌉ m so it stays ≤ 3000 × 3000 — a 3 km view is a 2 m grid): 1 m DTM GeoTIFF tiles (one UTM zone, EPSG 258xx / 326xx, Float32 or integer) plus any `.geojson` / `.kml` / `.kmz` overlays (polygons draped on the terrain, points as needles; a point with `role` = `steepest` is not drawn) and an optional `scene.json` (`title`, `lat`, `lon`, `radius`, `sigma` — the suggested smoothing in m, 0 = off).

- **Pick a place on the map** (the start screen; later, tap the coordinates or the 📍 in the panel's title): a Leaflet map with Kartverket's topo (loaded on demand); a **search box** above it finds a place by name — Norwegian names from Kartverket's place-name register (fuzzy; each hit with its kind and municipality), anything else from OpenStreetMap (Photon) — and a hit moves the map there and places the point (Enter takes the first); a tap sets the centre; the view's square is drawn (yellow; the 1 m detail square dashed white in the nested model); the view radius is typed in km, changed with Shift + mouse wheel over the map (the plain wheel zooms), or set by dragging the square's orange corner handle (the detail square's white one for the 1 m radius) — the red centre dot drags too; one-finger drag pans, pinch and + / − zoom the map; finger-sized controls on touch screens; the bar says how many tiles and MB the build fetches. The tiles come from the Geonorge WCS — 1 km tiles at 1 m, or 10 km tiles at 10 m, and coarser cells for a wide 1 m view (a 3 km view asks 2 m tiles, a quarter of the bytes) — one after another with exponential backoff (1 … 60 s), kept in the browser's Cache API (the last 120 tiles) so the next build of the same place is instant. Norway only. On a 10 m grid the contours are 20 m (bold every 100 m) instead of 5 / 25 m.
- **Three models.** *1 m*: Kartverket's 1 m model, radius ≤ 5 km (above 1.5 km the grid coarsens to ⌈2R / 3000⌉ m). *10 m*: the same model resampled by the server to 10 m, radius ≤ 15 km (contours 20 m / 100 m). *1 m centre + 10 m around*: a fine grid over ±R1 (≤ 5 km; above 1.5 km it coarsens like the 1 m view) nested in a coarse grid over ±R (≤ 15 km) — the surround is drawn with the centre cut out and the centre sits in the hole with a skirt, so no crack shows; a packaged folder with both `dtm_1m.tif` and `dtm_10m.tif` (`scene.json` `radius_1m`) opens the same way, and *1 m centre, radius* in the Data section moves the boundary. **The shading sees both grids:** the live sun shadows come from two shadow maps (one over the whole block, one at ≈ 0.8 m per pixel over the centre, each fed by the whole terrain, so the surround's ridges shade the centre); the sun-average horizons and the popup's "Sun now" are marched through the centre and on through the surround to the block's edge, with the Earth's curvature (less refraction) taken off distant heights; both grids share one colour range for the sun layer.
- **Load and Save, one path box** (Data section). **Load** reads whatever the box says: a web folder URL (needs an `index.json` = `{"files": ["dtm.tif", …]}` or a plain array, since a web server cannot list a directory; `?folder=<url>` opens it by link), the URL of a built `…_3dterrain.html` file (its embedded folder is read), a Google Drive link (sign-in follows), the name of a scene on this site (the box suggests them from `scenes/index.json` = `[{name, title, path}]`), or — box empty — a folder picked from the device (on a phone, files; a built `.html` among them is read as its folder). **Save** writes the current view, whatever it came from (a place built from the map, a folder, Drive), either as **one `<name>_3dterrain.html`** that opens offline (the page as served with the folder built in) or as a **folder `<name>/`** with the DTM tiles, the layer files (points), `scene.json` and `index.json` — what Load opens again from the device, a web server or Drive, and what you can annotate with more point lists or areas by dropping `.geojson` / `.kml` / `.kmz` files into it. The name comes from the box, else the view's title; Chrome and Edge ask where to put the file or folder, other browsers download the file or a zip of the folder.
- **Google Drive:** a shared Drive folder (or file) link in the path box → Load signs in (or open the viewer with `?drive=<link>`). Signs in with Google (read-only Drive access; token in memory only), lists the folder through the Drive API (sub-folders two levels down; no `index.json` needed) and reads the files with the token — so the data stays private to the accounts it is shared with. Needs the page opened from its web address (not a downloaded copy).
- **Sharing a terrain between two users:** **Share…** makes a link (`?drive=<folder link>` or `?folder=<url>`, plus the date and time). For a Drive folder, share the folder in Drive with the other person's Google account (Viewer); they open the link and sign in with their own account.
- **Base map** (selector): *Slope tint*, *Kartverket topo*, *Kartverket greyscale*, *OpenStreetMap* (drawn by OpenTopoMap), *Paths & cycling* (CyclOSM), *Streets (Esri)*, *Aerial (Esri)* *Aerial, earlier photos (Esri Wayback)* — the newest photo at once, then a *Photo* choice of every distinct earlier photo of the spot — or *Satellite, recent (NASA)*: a *Photo* choice of the days of the last 30 with a Sentinel-2 or Landsat pass over the view's centre (HLS, 30 m, from NASA GIBS, about a week behind; one small tile per day tells whether there was a pass — clouds are yours to judge) and the last three days of MODIS Terra (250 m, daily) — XYZ tiles for the view's box at the zoom whose pixel is about one grid cell, resampled onto the UTM grid so roads, streams and the map's own contours sit on the terrain; attribution along the bottom. A packaged folder can carry a map already draped for its view (`basemap_<id>.jpg`, `scene.json` `basemap`: {id, file}) — the standalone files carry Kartverket topo, so they work offline; the others need the network.
- **📍** in the title bar (and the coordinates themselves, hidden on a phone) opens the map to pick a new place.
- **⧉** in the title bar copies a link to the exact view: the terrain's link (place, web folder or Drive) plus only what departs from the defaults — layers switched on or off, hidden legend classes, the wind, snow and month choices of layers that are on, light mode, base map, height exaggeration, smoothing, and the camera when it has been moved — so an untouched view adds nothing; the link opens exactly so for the other person. *Share…* in the Data section still gives the plain terrain link.
- **Terrain features** section — the DTM-derived layers (Slope, Facing, Wind exposure and the masks below) sit in a collapsible *DTM derived* subpane, open by default; then the data layers (also *CORINE land cover 2018 (NIBIO)* — Norway's CORINE classes from NIBIO's WMS (EPSG:25833, one image per grid), blended at 55 % over the base map, with a legend of the classes in view and their shares (each pixel matched to the nearest standard CLC colour); hovering names the class the same way, a tap on the terrain asks the WMS for the exact class (English and Norwegian name, code); needs the network, not packaged into saved files; then four collapsible groups of **NIBIO** layers — *SR16 forest resources* (16 m cells: tree species, mean and dominant height, crown cover, leaf area index, timber volume, above-ground biomass, site index, mean stem diameter, basal area, trees per hectare), *AR5 land resources* (land type, tree species, forest productivity, ground conditions), *Vegetation types* (full and main types — mountain and outfield areas only) and *MIS forest habitats* (key biotopes, hollow broadleaf trees, old trees, standing and lying dead wood, older broadleaf succession, cliff walls, stream gorges, clay ravines, rich ground vegetation, trees with hanging lichen, rich-bark trees, burnt areas, surveyed area) — each layer its own toggle; the map-filling ones (SR16, AR5, vegetation) are on one at a time at 55 %, the MIS features stack on top as drawn; the legend lists the classes present in the view with their colours and shares (largest first; a layer with nothing in view has no entry; a click on a class hides or shows it, a click on a MIS entry switches that layer off) (SR16's transcribed from NIBIO's legend images, the vector layers' from the service's style sheet; shown in English) and the class is named under the pointer, the MIS layers by which of them have something under it; a tap asks each service for its values at the point (SR16: the cell's values of all eleven, heights in m), attribution names the groups on; needs the network, not packaged; and *Snow depth (seNorge, NVE)* on a chosen day — any day since 1957 and the next nine (forecast) — and *Snow days > 5 cm (seNorge, NVE)* for a chosen year (the last complete year by default): NVE's 1 km seNorge grids from gridwms.nve.no, one image per grid, blended at 55 %, seNorge's own palette as the legend, the class under the pointer and in the tap popup; a day or year with no grid says so) — *Slope* in NVE's steepness classes (27–30 · 30–35 · 35–40 · 40–45 · ≥ 45°); *Facing* — the way slopes ≥ 15° face, eight directions, one colour each — in both legends a click on a class's swatch hides or shows that class alone (dimmed and struck through while hidden); *Wind exposure* — Winstral's shelter index on the sun model's coarse cells: for each cell the steepest line of sight to higher ground upwind within 300 m, as an angle, positive where higher ground stands upwind (sheltered, blue), negative where the ground falls away (exposed, red), the colours spread over the view's own range (red at its 1st percentile, pale at its median, blue at its 99th; the three numbers are in the legend); the wind is chosen next to it — *all directions* alike, one compass point, or *the winds here*: the view centre's own wind rose from Open-Meteo's ERA5 archive (the last three years' days, each day's dominant direction weighted by its peak speed; the three main directions and their shares are written under the legend; needs the network) — the value and its reading (exposed / sheltered / neither) under the pointer; then masks worked out from the model, painted at 70 % in their colour, named under the pointer: *Ridges and crests* and *Gullies and ravines* (ground more than 3 m above / below its surroundings within 25 m, cliff faces left out), *Ledges on cliffs* (a near-flat step with cliff within 3 m above and below; 1 m grids), *Cliff drops* — one toggle that opens five classes under it, the rows reading 5–10, 10–15, 15–25, 25–50 and ≥ 50 m, each with its own tick and colour, all on when the toggle goes on, hidden when it goes off (from every cell whose steepest step is ≥ 60° the face is followed up and down its steepest line; its drop over its horizontal run, less two cells on 1 m grids for the gridding's smear of a vertical wall, must be ≥ 85°; unsmoothed heights; the face's cells coloured solid over the contours, not blended, plus the first tile beyond its top and its bottom; 1 m views in practice), *Scree and boulder fields* (rough ground on 15–40° slopes; 1 m grids), *Lakes and ponds* (level ground above sea level in patches ≥ 400 m² — the model is flat on water), *Updraft slopes* (≥ 20° facing south-west to north-west), *Streams* (D8 flow accumulation over the smoothed, pit-filled model: channels draining ≥ 1 ha; the view's own square only), *Mires* (ground < 3° with a topographic wetness index ≥ 9, lakes left out), *Alpine plateaus* (mean slope over 50 m < 8° above a tree line estimated from the latitude alone, the estimate written in the row), *Viewshed* (the ground in sight from 1 m over the centre or over a tapped point — the point popup's *Viewshed* button; the share of the view seen, and km², in the row; carried in the view link), and from the sun model (computed when first switched on) *Sun-warmed slopes in spring* (the sunniest quarter in March–April on slopes ≥ 5°), *Shade hollows* (the least-sunny fifth over the year, in hollows: late snow) and *Sheltered ground* (the quarter with the least open sky). The thresholds are plain starting points, not calibrated habitat models. Then four graded layers, each a value per cell painted at 68 % over the view's 1st–99th percentile range (a legend bar with the numbers, the value under the pointer): *Topographic openness* (Yokoyama's positive openness within 500 m: 90° on level ground, more on crests, less in hollows), *Distance to ridge or cliff edge* (exact Euclidean distance to the nearest crest or brink; fixed 0–250 m colours), *Ruggedness, TRI* (Riley: mean height difference to the eight neighbours) and *Ruggedness, VRM* (Sappington: disagreement of the surface normals within 5 m). Not yet: coastal wave exposure.
- The square on the picker map follows the model's UTM grid (zone 33), so it is turned a few degrees against north — the status line says how much; the 3-D block is cut along that grid, and the compass shows true north.
- **Shape:** a block with side walls (the whole square, four walls down to a base below the lowest point, closed underneath), lit by the sun.
- **Ground view** (beside Reset view / Top view, and *Ground view* in the tap popup's button row): the camera stands 1.75 m above the ground at the point the view turns about (or the tapped point) and looks level at the horizon in the current heading; rotate to look round, tilt to look down, drag to walk. All overlays are the terrain's paint, so every one shows.
- **Help**: the **?** in the title opens a sectioned help (getting a terrain, moving around, map, terrain features, sun, pointing and measuring, data and sharing); a **?** behind each terrain feature opens it at that feature's entry — what it shows and the assumptions made. The wind-exposure entry explains its degrees: the elevation angle up to the highest ground within 300 m upwind (+30° a wall, 0° level, −20° the ground falling away: a crest).
- **2D / 3D**: the *2D* button in the control column shows the same place as a flat Leaflet map carrying the terrain's layers — every layer, mask and graded layer, pins and areas, but not the base map nor the contours (the map has its own) — warped from the UTM square onto Web Mercator (one image per grid, the surround under the 1 m square). The *Map* dropdown is shared: *Plain* is a relief shaded by the light in use with nothing outside the fetched square, any tile map gets the layers drawn over it with transparency (the layers are painted on a white and a black base and the alpha recovered, so the paint code is one). Hover, the pinned short tap, the long press / right-click popup, pins and areas work as in 3-D; *Ground view* from the map returns to the terrain there; *3D* returns centred where the map was. View links carry the flat state.
- **Map** section: the base map, *Height ×* and *Smooth terrain* (the model's shape), *Contours* (5 m, labelled every 25 m), one layer **per loaded overlay file** (see below), *Centre & circles*, *Bold markers*. **Sun** section: *Sun: average direct radiation* — clear-sky direct sun on the ground in kWh/m² per day, averaged hourly over two days of each month (NOAA sun position, beam × cos incidence on the slope, 0 below the cell's own horizon — 32 directions marched over the terrain in view), for a **month range** set with a two-handle slider (Jan – Apr until changed; the range is remembered on the device); one layer **per loaded overlay file** (`scene.json` `layers`: {file: {title, legend: [[colour, label] …], group: "birds"}} names and explains it — `group: "birds"` makes it start switched off, as the cliff layer does). The compass (true north) is always shown. Hovering shows the height, slope, facing and — with the sun layer — that point's average. **A long press (touch) or right-click (mouse) on the terrain** opens a popup with the point's coordinates (tap them to copy) and height; a **short tap** pins the hover text (height, slope, facing, every overlay's value) at the point until the next tap — the overlays' values on a touch screen; the model with *Fetch+* and *Fetch* on one line, *Ground view* and *Viewshed* on the next. The **🐦** control opens BirdsWhere (thebirding.site) centred on the view; the **🛰** control opens norgeskart.no there with Kartverket's orthophoto.
- **Missing DTM** (tiles that do not exist): filled with the smoothest surface that meets the known ground (least squares, solved coarse to fine), **greyed and hatched**; the panel gives its share and hovering says "No DTM data here".
- **Shown:** contours, slope tint, the overlays, a centre pin with 500 m / radius circles, a compass, a height slider (×1–3, ×1 by default), map-style controls (drag moves the map; rotate by twisting two fingers, right-drag / Shift+drag, the ✋ → ⟳ drag-mode button, or the ⟲ ⟳ ▲ ▼ buttons, N = north up; wheel / pinch / +− zoom, arrow keys pan), top view; a **📏 measuring tool** (tap two points: horizontal distance, height difference, distance along the line, slope in ° and %, true bearing); **optional smoothing** (☑ Smooth terrain, σ m — on at 1.5 m by default; off shows the raw 1 m model). It opens looking down at the area, in daylight (at night: today's solar noon).
- **Sun:** date and time sliders (Norway time, CET/CEST), the NOAA solar-position equations → directional light with cast terrain shadows; sun elevation / azimuth, sunrise / solar noon / sunset; hovering shows the clear-sky direct irradiance on the surface (W/m², incidence on the 1 m slope, terrain shadow) and the day's direct total (kWh/m²). "▶ Day" animates the day.
- **Panels:** one column down the left side — height, smoothing and the view buttons on top, then the collapsible sections **Map**, **Terrain features**, **Sun** and **Data** (the path box with Load / Save, the model, view radius and detail radius as text, centre, Render, Share); a closed section's triangle turns **blue** when a toggle inside it is away from its default (a layer on, contours off …), as in BirdsWhere; **‹** tucks the column away. The map controls on the right are one column too. The info panel minimises.
- **Pins:** hovering a point's or an area's pin shows its name and description; tapping it opens the popup with that text, the coordinates and the height. **Bold markers** makes the pins twice the size, draws them over the terrain, and outlines the areas in white.

## The scripts

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt          # numpy scipy rasterio pyproj shapely affine Pillow (pyogrio only with N250)
cp config.example.toml config.toml       # optional: where the tile caches (and N250) live

python3 viz3d.py 58.98634 6.19051 --name preikestolen --radius 1000 --title "Preikestolen (demo)"
python3 viz3d.py LAT LON --name NAME --radius R [--res 1|10] [--radius-1m R1] [--points FILE.geojson] [--site DIR] [--out DIR]
python3 rewrap.py FILE_3dterrain.html … [--points FILE.geojson] [--viewer DIR]
```

**`viz3d.py LAT LON`** packages the ±R square round a point:

1. fetches the 1 km tiles of Kartverket's 1 m model it does not have yet (Geonorge WCS, one at a time, exponential backoff 1 … 60 s) into the tile cache, and mosaics them;
2. runs the cliff rules (`lib/habitat.py`; every constant in `rules.json`): steep ground ≥ 50° on the σ 1.5 m-smoothed model, kept as a **cliff** when its face is ≥ 10 m high and looks E … S … W, each with its height, facing, shape, and (with N250) distance to the nearest building and road; steep ground inside an N250 quarry as **quarry**; small islands (< 20 ha, no building) as **islet**; nothing within 25 m of the data's edge. Coloured by facing (S strongest);
3. drapes Kartverket's topo map onto the view's grid (tiles cached) so the standalone file works offline;
4. writes `site/scenes/<name>/` — `dtm_1m.tif` (the raw model, cm precision, EPSG:25833; on a ⌈2R / 3000⌉ m grid beyond 1.5 km) — or, with `--res 10`, `dtm_10m.tif` over ±R (≤ 15 km, from the WCS's 10 km tiles or a local DTM10, `config.toml` `dtm10`; no habitat), plus with `--radius-1m R1` the 1 m model and its habitat over ±R1 (`scene.json` `radius_1m`: the viewer nests the two) — `basemap_kv_topo.jpg`, `habitat.geojson`, `points.geojson` (the steepest point on a nesting cliff + your `--points` within the radius), `scene.json`, `index.json` — and adds it to `site/scenes/index.json`, regenerates `site/index.html`;
5. writes `<out>/<name>_3dterrain.html`: the viewer with that folder built in (base64) — one file, opens from the disk, offline.

`--points FILE.geojson` is a FeatureCollection of points (properties `name`, `desc`, `role`, `color`); a top-level `"layer"` member `{title, legend: [[colour, label] …], group}` names its layer in the viewer. Private points stay in your own files: the repo holds none.

**`rewrap.py`** puts the current viewer round already-built standalone files, keeping each file's embedded folder (no recomputation) — after a viewer change. `--points` swaps the points in each file for those of the file within its view; `--viewer DIR` also rewrites the empty viewer `DIR/index.html`.

**`config.toml`** (`lib/config.py`; `$TERRAIN3D_CONFIG`, `./config.toml`, `~/.config/terrain3d/config.toml`): `dtm1_tiles` and `map_tiles` — the caches (default under `~/.cache/terrain3d/`; the 10 m tiles go to `tiles10/` beside the 1 m `tiles/`); `dtm10` — a local DTM10 instead of the WCS at 10 m, optional; `n250` — Kartverket's N250 file geodatabase, optional.
